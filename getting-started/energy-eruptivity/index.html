    Energy and eruptivity¶

    How much energy can the region release, and is it close to erupting? This page computes the usual answers from an extrapolation. It starts from the models pot and nl of An HMI active region. The equations are in Method.

    Energy and helicity¶

    rep = sff.helicity_report(nl)
e, h = rep["energy"], rep["helicity"]
e["E"], e["E_p"], e["E_free"], e["E_J"], e["E_div_frac"]     # erg
h["H_V"], h["H_J"], h["ratio_HJ_HV"]                          # Mx^2
rep["gauge_diff"]                                              # numerical error of the helicity

    Contents of the result of helicity_report. Quantity: the key in rep["energy"], rep["helicity"] or rep. Meaning: what it is. Energies are in erg and helicities in Mx².

    Quantity Meaning
    E, E_p energy of the model and of the potential field with the same flux on the six faces
    E_free free energy, E - E_p: the most that a relaxation to the potential field could release
    E_J energy of the current-carrying part, another definition of the free energy
    E_div_frac fraction of the energy that is spurious, due to the residual divergence (Valori et al. 2013)
    H_V, H_J relative helicity, and its current-carrying part
    ratio_HJ_HV eruptivity index of Pariat et al. (2017)
    gauge_diff relative difference between the helicity computed in two gauges: the larger, the less reliable the helicity

    Energy decomposition and relative helicity of the linear and the NLFFF models. The NLFFF stores most of its free energy in the current-carrying part.

    State which free energy you quote

    E_free and E_J differ by 4–19 % in the regions studied. In a paper say which one you quote, and give E_div_frac, which tells how much of the energy comes from the residual divergence (Thalmann et al. 2019 discuss the thresholds).

    Twist¶

    tw = sff.twist_map(nl, step=2)          # twist number of the line leaving each pixel
tw["tw"], tw["closed"], tw["length"]

    The twist number \(T_w\) counts how many turns the field lines make around each other along a line (Berger & Prior 2006; Liu et al. 2016). \(\lvert T_w\rvert \gtrsim 1\) suggests a flux rope.

    \(T_w\) of the closed line leaving each pixel, for the linear and the NLFFF models. The black line is the polarity inversion line.

    Decay index and torus instability¶

    pot2 = sff.potential_field(mag, nz=48, pad=2)        # pad=2: less influence of the periodic copies
d = sff.decay_index_profile(pot2)
d["z"], d["n"], d["h_crit"]                          # profile n(z) and the height where n = 1.5

    The decay index \(n = -\,d\ln B_h / d\ln z\) of the potential field above the inversion line measures how fast the confining field falls with height. A flux rope above the height where \(n\) reaches about 1.5 can erupt by the torus instability (Kliem & Török 2006).

    Left: \(n(z)\) above the inversion line and the critical height. Right: the columns where it is measured.

    Quasi-separatrix layers¶

    q = sff.squashing_q(nl, step=2)
q["logq"]                    # log10 of the squashing factor at the base

    The squashing factor \(Q\) measures how fast the connectivity changes from one footpoint to the next (Titov et al. 2002). Thin layers with \(Q \gg 2\) are quasi-separatrix layers, where reconnection and flare ribbons tend to occur.

    \(\log_{10} Q\) at the base for the potential and the NLFFF models. Dark bands are QSLs; grey where it is undefined.

    Currents along the lines¶

    sff.plot_alpha_histogram({"Potential": pot, "LFFF": lin, "NLFFF": nl}, seeds)

    Left: the mean α of each line. Right: how much α varies along it. In an exact force-free field it is constant along each line, so the spread is a measure of how force-free the model really is.

    The parameters¶

    Parameters of the eruptivity diagnostics. Function: the call they belong to; an empty cell repeats the one above. Parameter, Default: its name and value if not given. What it does: its effect.

    Function Parameter Default What it does
    twist_map, squashing_q step 1 one line every step pixels of the base
    threshold 0.05 only where \(\lvert B_z\rvert\) exceeds this fraction of the maximum
    ds 0.25, 0.2 integration step, in cells
    squashing_q delta 0.25 displacement of the neighbouring lines, in cells
    decay_index_profile mask None columns where \(n\) is measured; by default above the inversion line
    n_crit 1.5 critical value of the decay index
    potential_field pad 1 padding of the Fourier domain; 2 for the decay index

    A smaller step gives finer maps and takes longer, roughly as the square.
