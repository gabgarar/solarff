    Method and equations¶

    This page puts the library in context: the physical problem, the equations each step solves, and what goes in and comes out of every function.

    The problem¶

    The magnetic field of the solar corona stores the energy released in flares and eruptions, but it cannot be measured directly. What is measured routinely is the vector field at the photosphere. The coronal field is then extrapolated from that boundary, assuming that in the low corona the magnetic force dominates and the field is close to force-free:

    \[ (\nabla\times\mathbf{B})\times\mathbf{B} = 0, \qquad \nabla\cdot\mathbf{B} = 0 . \]

    The first equation says the electric current is parallel to the field, so it can be written with a scalar, the force-free parameter \(\alpha\):

    \[ \nabla\times\mathbf{B} = \alpha\,\mathbf{B}, \qquad \alpha = \frac{(\nabla\times\mathbf{B})\cdot\mathbf{B}}{B^2}, \qquad \mathbf{B}\cdot\nabla\alpha = 0 . \]

    The last relation follows from taking the divergence: in an exact solution \(\alpha\) is constant along each field line. Three models of increasing realism follow from what is assumed about \(\alpha\).

    Model Assumption Boundary data needed
    Potential \(\alpha = 0\) (no currents) \(B_z\)
    Linear force-free (LFFF) \(\alpha\) constant everywhere \(B_z\) and one value of \(\alpha\)
    Nonlinear force-free (NLFFF) \(\alpha\) varies from line to line \(B_x\), \(B_y\), \(B_z\)

    The NLFFF is the realistic one, but it is an ill-posed numerical problem: the photosphere is not force-free, the side and top boundaries are unknown, and the data are noisy. The result is a field that is good in some places and wrong in others. The per-line confidence is the tool to tell them apart.

    Step 1: preprocessing¶

    The photospheric field is not force-free, so it is not a consistent boundary for a force-free solution. A consistent boundary must have zero net force and zero net torque (Aly 1989). The library measures the departure with

    \[ \epsilon_F = \frac{\left|\sum B_xB_z\right| + \left|\sum B_yB_z\right| + \left|\sum (B_z^2 - B_x^2 - B_y^2)\right|}{\sum (B_x^2+B_y^2+B_z^2)} , \]

    and an analogous \(\epsilon_T\) for the torque (aly_criteria). Following Wiegelmann, Inhester & Sakurai (2006), the magnetogram is modified slightly by minimising

    \[ L_{\rm pre} = \mu_1 L_1 + \mu_2 L_2 + \mu_3 L_3 + \mu_4 L_4 , \]

    where \(L_1\) is the net force, \(L_2\) the net torque, \(L_3 = \sum|\mathbf{B}-\mathbf{B}_{\rm obs}|^2\) the distance to the data and \(L_4 = \sum|\Delta\mathbf{B}|^2\) the smoothness. Each term is normalised by its mean, so the weights do not depend on the size or strength of the magnetogram.

    Parameter Default Meaning
    mu1, mu2 1 weight of the force and torque terms
    mu3 10⁻² how far the result may move from the data
    mu4 10⁻² how much it is smoothed
    fix_bz False modify only the transverse components

    Warning

    The confidence is calibrated with these defaults. Changing mu3 or mu4 degrades it without notice.

    Step 2: extrapolation¶

    Potential and linear fields are solved in Fourier space (Alissandrakis 1981). Each horizontal mode \(\mathbf{k}\) of \(B_z\) at the base decays with height as \(e^{-\sqrt{k^2-\alpha^2}\,z}\), which requires \(|\alpha| < 2\pi/L\) for a box of side \(L\). potential_field(mag, nz) is the case \(\alpha = 0\), and lfff(mag, alpha, nz) the general one.

    The NLFFF minimises a functional over the volume (Wheatland et al. 2000; Wiegelmann 2004):

    \[ \begin{aligned} L ={}& \left\langle w\left[\frac{|(\nabla\times\mathbf{B})\times\mathbf{B}|^2}{B^2+\varepsilon^2} + w_d\,|\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B}|^2\right]\right\rangle_V \\ &+ \nu\left\langle W\,|\mathbf{B}_t-\mathbf{B}_{t,\rm obs}|^2\right\rangle_{z=0} + \lambda\left\langle|\nabla^2\mathbf{B}|^2\right\rangle_V . \end{aligned} \]

    The first term is the force and the divergence, the second lets the transverse field at the base adjust within the noise (Wiegelmann & Inhester 2010), and the third penalises grid-scale oscillations. The weighting function \(w\) is 1 in the interior and falls to 0 in a buffer layer of nd cells next to the side and top boundaries, which are fixed to the potential field. The gradient is exact, by automatic differentiation, and the minimiser is L-BFGS.

    Parameter of nlfff_lbfgs Default Symbol and meaning
    nz required number of grid levels in height
    nd max(4, min(nx, ny, nz)/10) width of the buffer layer, in cells
    relax_bottom 0.1 \(\nu\), freedom of the transverse field at the base
    eps 0.03 \(\varepsilon\) in units of max abs(\(B_z\)), regularisation near nulls
    smooth 10⁻² \(\lambda\), smoothness
    div_weight 1 \(w_d\), weight of the divergence term
    max_iter, tol 2000, 10⁻⁶ stopping criteria

    The output is a VectorField3D. Its quality is summarised by the current-weighted angle between \(\mathbf{J}\) and \(\mathbf{B}\),

    \[ \mathrm{CW}_{\sin} = \frac{\sum_i |\mathbf{J}_i|\,\sigma_i}{\sum_i |\mathbf{J}_i|}, \qquad \sigma_i = \frac{|\mathbf{J}_i\times\mathbf{B}_i|}{|\mathbf{J}_i|\,|\mathbf{B}_i|}, \]

    which is 0 for an exactly force-free field, and by the fractional flux \(\langle|f_i|\rangle\), which measures the residual divergence.

    Step 3: field lines¶

    A field line is the solution of \(d\mathbf{r}/ds = \mathbf{B}/|\mathbf{B}|\). It is integrated in both directions from a seed with fourth-order Runge–Kutta on the trilinearly interpolated field.

    Parameter of trace_field_lines Default Meaning
    seeds required array (N, 3) of starting points, in Mm
    ds 0.5 integration step, in cells
    max_steps 4000 maximum steps per direction

    seeds_from_magnetogram(mag, n, threshold, polarity) places n seeds where abs(\(B_z\)) exceeds threshold times its maximum. Each FieldLine holds its points in Mm, its seed and whether both ends are at the base (closed).

    Step 4: indicators and confidence¶

    Three quantities are computed on each extrapolated line, with no knowledge of the true field.

    Indicator Definition Why it reveals a wrong line
    Variation of α \(x_1 = \log_{10}\) of the spread of \(\alpha\) along the line, relative to its mean \(\alpha\) must be constant along a line of an exact solution
    Buffer fraction \(x_2\) = fraction of the line inside the buffer layer there the field is set by unobserved boundaries
    Length \(x_3 = \log_{10}(L/D)\), with \(D\) the horizontal diagonal of the box, and \(x_4 = (x_3 + 0.54)^2\) long lines accumulate error; very short ones fail in flux ropes

    They are combined in a logistic regression. The probability that a line is wrong is

    \[ p = \frac{1}{1+\exp\left[-\left(\beta_0+\sum_i \beta_i z_i\right)\right]}, \qquad z_i = \frac{x_i-\mu_i}{\sigma_i}, \]

    and the confidence is \(1 - p\). The published coefficients, with \(\beta_0 = -1.33\), are:

    Term \(x_i\) \(\mu_i\) \(\sigma_i\) \(\beta_i\)
    \(\log_{10}\) (variation of α) −0.788 0.606 +0.79
    buffer fraction 0.166 0.277 +0.79
    \(\log_{10}(L/D)\) −0.543 0.313 +1.12
    \((\log_{10}(L/D)+0.54)^2\) 0.098 0.162 +0.78

    The whole model fits in this table, so it can be applied without the library.

    Output of assess_lines(field, lines, mag):

    Key Content
    confidence array with one value in [0, 1] per line
    reliable boolean array, confidence ≥ threshold
    threshold the acceptance threshold, 0.8 by default
    indicators dictionary with every indicator of every line

    How the coefficients were obtained¶

    A line is labelled wrong by comparing it with the exact line traced from the same seed. Both are parametrised by normalised arc length \(s\) and the shape error is

    \[ \epsilon = \frac{1}{L_{\rm ref}}\int_0^1 \left|\mathbf{r}_{\rm NLFFF}(s)-\mathbf{r}_{\rm ref}(s)\right|ds . \]

    A line is wrong if \(\epsilon > 0.1\) or if it changes between open and closed. The model is then judged by:

    Measure Definition Question it answers
    AUC probability that a wrong line gets a higher \(p\) than a good one does it rank the lines?
    ECE \(\sum_m \frac{\lvert B_m\rvert}{n}\,\lvert\bar y(B_m)-\bar p(B_m)\rvert\) over 10 bins of \(p\) can the probability be read literally?
    Brier score \(\frac{1}{n}\sum_i (p_i-y_i)^2\) both at once
    Risk–coverage fraction of wrong lines among those kept, against the fraction kept which threshold suits my use?

    Step 5: energy, helicity and topology¶

    With \(\mathbf{B}_p\) the potential field that has the same normal component on the six faces of the box, the energy is decomposed following Valori et al. (2013). Two free energies are given:

    \[ E_{\rm free} = E - E_p, \qquad E_J = \frac{1}{8\pi}\int_V |\mathbf{B}-\mathbf{B}_p|^2\,dV , \]

    which coincide only if the field is solenoidal. The fraction \(E_{\rm div}/E\) measures how much of the energy is spurious because of the residual divergence.

    The relative magnetic helicity (Finn & Antonsen 1985) is

    \[ H_V = \int_V (\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{A}_p)\cdot(\mathbf{B}-\mathbf{B}_p)\,dV , \]

    with the vector potentials in the DeVore gauge. It is computed with two reference faces, and the difference between them is the numerical error of the helicity.

    Output of helicity_report(field):

    Key Content Units
    energy E, E_p, E_free, E_J, E_div, E_div_frac and the other terms of the decomposition erg
    helicity H_V, H_J, H_PJ and ratio_HJ_HV, gauge referred to the top face Mx²
    helicity_bottom_gauge the same, gauge referred to the bottom face Mx²
    gauge_diff relative difference between the two gauges —
    potential the reference potential field \(\mathbf{B}_p\) G

    Three more diagnostics describe whether a region can erupt:

    Quantity Definition Function
    Twist number \(T_w = \dfrac{1}{4\pi}\displaystyle\int \alpha\,dl\) along each line twist_map, twist_number
    Decay index \(n(z) = -\dfrac{d\ln B_h}{d\ln z}\) of the potential field above the inversion line decay_index_profile
    Squashing factor \(Q\), how strongly the line mapping between footpoints distorts a small circle squashing_q

    A flux rope is usually taken as abs(\(T_w\)) ≳ 1, torus instability as \(n \geq 1.5\), and a quasi-separatrix layer as \(Q \gg 2\).

    Units and conventions¶

    • Field in gauss, lengths in megametres, energy in erg, helicity in Mx².
    • Axes: \(x\) westward, \(y\) northward, \(z\) vertical. From SHARP: \(B_x = B_p\), \(B_y = -B_t\), \(B_z = B_r\).
    • The geometry is Cartesian, with no curvature, valid for regions up to about 200–300 Mm.
